Allergic Reaction Protocol

1. Take any guest report of an allergic reaction seriously, even if symptoms seem mild at first.
2. Ask the guest immediately: What did you eat? Do you have an EpiPen? Are you feeling throat tightness, difficulty breathing, or swelling?
3. Call emergency services (911) at once if the guest reports any breathing difficulty, throat swelling, loss of consciousness, or severe hives.
4. If the guest has an EpiPen and consents, assist or remind them to use it immediately. Do not administer it yourself unless trained.
5. Keep the guest seated, calm, and still. Lay them down if they feel faint. Do not give them water or food.
6. Remove all food and beverages from the table to prevent further exposure.
7. Notify the manager or shift lead immediately and remain with the guest until help arrives.
8. Preserve the dish, packaging, and any remaining ingredients from the meal for review.
9. Document the guest's name (if provided), the dish ordered, symptoms observed, and the time of onset.
10. Do not make assumptions or assign fault publicly. Do not comment to other guests about the incident.
11. After the incident, review ingredient lists, kitchen cross-contamination procedures, and allergen labeling.
12. File a full incident report for management and ownership review.
